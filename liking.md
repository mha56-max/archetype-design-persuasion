# Liking

[Home](https://github.com/mha56-max/archetype-design-persuasion/blob/main/README.md)

## What it is

Liking is a persuasion principle from Robert Cialdini. People are more likely to say yes to someone they like. Cialdini points to several things that build liking: similarity, genuine compliments, cooperation toward a shared goal, familiarity, and attractiveness.

Liking is about warmth and personal appeal. It differs from Unity, which is about shared group identity.

## Recognize and apply it

Liking shows up when a message feels friendly, relatable, and personal, as if it comes from someone the audience would enjoy talking to. In design and communication, it can be applied by:

* Using a warm, conversational tone.
* Showing people and situations the audience sees as similar to themselves.
* Offering honest praise or appreciation to the audience.
* Using humor and positivity that make the brand feel approachable.
* Showing the brand working with the audience toward the same goal.

## Example 1: Tupperware home parties

Cialdini describes Tupperware parties in *Influence* as a classic case of Liking. Customers buy from a friend or acquaintance who hosts the party, so the relationship, not the product alone, drives the purchase.

**How Liking is used:** People respond to someone they already like and trust, so the sale feels like helping a friend.

## Example 2: Dove

Dove's "Real Beauty" communication features ordinary people instead of models. This can make the audience feel the brand understands them and shares their point of view.

**How Liking is used:** The audience sees people similar to themselves, which builds familiarity and warmth toward the brand.
**Source:** [Dove](https://www.dove.com/)

## How I would use Liking

I would use Liking by making the brand feel like a person the audience already enjoys. I would use friendly wording, relatable images, and honest appreciation of the audience instead of hard-sell language.

For the Everyman, Lover, and Jester archetypes, Liking works in different ways. For the Everyman, it highlights being down-to-earth and "just like you." For the Lover, it highlights warmth, affection, and making people feel appreciated. For the Jester, it uses humor and playfulness to make the brand fun to be around.
