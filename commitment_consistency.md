# Commitment and Consistency

[Home](https://github.com/mha56-max/archetype-design-persuasion/blob/main/README.md)

## What it is

Commitment and Consistency is a persuasion principle from Robert Cialdini. People want their actions to match what they have already said or done. Once someone makes a small commitment, they are more likely to follow through with larger ones that fit it.

## Recognize and apply it

This principle appears when a brand asks for a small first step and then builds on it. In design and communication, it can be applied by:

* Starting with a small, easy request such as signing up, choosing a goal, or taking a short quiz.
* Asking people to state a goal or preference in their own words.
* Reminding people of what they already chose or started.
* Rewarding progress and streaks so people keep going.
* Making it easy to stay consistent with their earlier choice.

The "foot-in-the-door" technique is a well-known case. Freedman and Fraser (1966) found that people who agreed to a small request were more likely to agree to a larger one later.

## Example 1: Duolingo

Duolingo asks new users to choose a daily learning goal, then tracks streaks of consecutive days. Having set a goal and built a streak, users feel motivated to keep it going.

**How Commitment and Consistency is used:** The small first commitment and visible streak encourage people to act consistently with their earlier choice.
**Source:** [Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com/)

## Example 2: Free trials and sign-ups

Many subscription services start with a free trial or a simple account sign-up. Once a person has created an account and started using the product, continuing feels consistent with their earlier decision.

**How Commitment and Consistency is used:** A low-stakes first step leads to a larger commitment later.

## How I would use Commitment and Consistency

I would use it by giving the audience a small, easy first action in the hero section, then reinforcing that choice in follow-up messages.

For the Everyman, Lover, and Jester archetypes, it works differently. For the Everyman, it means simple, no-pressure first steps that are easy for anyone. For the Lover, it means building loyalty and a lasting relationship through small shared moments. For the Jester, it means a playful, low-stakes first step, like a fun quiz, that people want to keep doing.
